# The Problem Lahore Faces

## Air Pollution Kills — But Resources for Response Are Limited

Lahore, Pakistan's second-largest city (population ~13 million), regularly ranks among the world's most polluted cities. In winter 2024, PM2.5 levels regularly exceeded **10× the WHO guideline** of 15 µg/m³, with some monitoring stations recording **200+ µg/m³** — categorized as "Hazardous."

## The Real Challenge Is Not Measurement — It's Response

Lahore already has **air quality monitoring stations**. The gap is not data collection. The gap is **what to do with the data when a pollution event occurs**.

When an abnormal pollution episode is detected:

- **Which neighborhoods** are most exposed?
- **Which direction** is the pollution plume moving?
- **Which investigation areas** should response officers visit first?
- **What happened before** in similar situations, and what was found?

Today, these decisions rely on individual officer experience, manual AQI checks, and phone calls. There is no systematic way to convert air quality data into **actionable investigation recommendations**.

## The Consequence

- Response teams deploy to the wrong areas first
- Limited enforcement resources are spread thin across 13 million people
- Repeat pollution sources go undetected because no one connects the dots between episodes
- The same investigation mistakes are repeated across different episodes
- Citizens have no way to understand what the data means for their health

## What's Missing

A system that can:

1. **Detect** abnormal pollution events automatically
2. **Assemble** the environmental context around each event
3. **Constrain** AI analysis to only what the evidence supports
4. **Recommend** specific investigation areas with a confidence level
5. **Predict** approximate exposure direction
6. **Learn** from what human officers actually found
7. **Explain** exactly what it can and cannot determine
